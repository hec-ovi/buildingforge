# CONTRACT: exterior

Purpose: deterministically generates one building exterior as a GLB shell (empty inside, one separator plane per floor) plus a JSON blueprint of every exterior opening per floor.

Status: v0.46.11, implemented. Schemas are stable to build against; additive fields preserve compatibility and breaking changes go through the orchestrator.

## Conventions
- Units: meters. Ground plane XZ, +Y up, right-handed. 2D points `[x, z]`, CCW rings, first point not repeated (same as atlas).
- Outputs share the request footprint's coordinate frame; ground floor walking surface at Y = 0.
- Determinism: same request, same materials database and same texture options give a byte-identical GLB and blueprint JSON. No LLM, no wall clock, no ambient randomness.
- Environment: Node reads `URBE_MATERIALS_DIR` only as the default materials box path. An explicit `options.textures.dir` takes precedence. The browser preview uses its supplied HTTP source.

## In
`generate(request: BuildingRequest, options?: GenerateOptions): Promise<{ glb: Uint8Array, blueprint: Blueprint, textures: { mode, reason? } }>`

Request: [schemas/building-request.schema.json](schemas/building-request.schema.json). Seed, parcel (footprint, street access point, max height), building (atlas type and tier verbatim, floor count, basements, optional per-floor kind labels), theme slug, required apertures (connections' aperture schema verbatim: face = parcel footprint segment index, absolute base, world-space cut polygon; kinds bridge, ac-tube, tunnel, wire-anchor), options (shape, balconies, balcony style, open frontage, fire escape, windows, signage marquee/logo, ad screens, roof artifacts, curtain profile and per-opening open percentage, facade services, hanging clothes, sparse window damage).

`options.textures`: `{ mode?: "external" | "embed" | "keys", dir?, baseUrl?, source? }`. `dir` is the materials box root (defaults to `URBE_MATERIALS_DIR`, else the sibling `materials` box), `baseUrl` is the URI prefix written into the GLB before `themes/<theme>/assets/...`, and `source` is a preloaded materials source for callers with no filesystem (the browser preview) or `null` to force the keys fallback.

CLI: `npm run generate -- <request.json> <outDir> [--seed S] [--embed | --keys-only] [--materials DIR] [--materials-base URI]` writes `<buildingId>.glb` and `<buildingId>.blueprint.json`; external map URIs are written relative to the output directory by default.

Seeds: `generate` needs one, so a request with no seed (and no `--seed`) gets a random one rolled at the edge. The CLI prints the seed it used on every run and the preview keeps it in the seed field, so any building can be regenerated exactly.

Feasibility: [schemas/floor-constants.json](schemas/floor-constants.json) carries the per-type constants the generator enforces (type-to-family map, min and max floor height, min footprint area) plus the recipe for pre-computing a guaranteed-feasible floor count, with or without apertures.

Proportions: [schemas/proportions.json](schemas/proportions.json) is the published opening size table, also exported as `PROPORTIONS`. A floor's clear height is its floor-to-floor height minus `clearHeightAllowance`. Entrances stand by family: residential 2.4 to 2.9 m, venues (restaurant, coffee shop, commerce, mall) and hospitals 3.2 to 3.8 m, hotel and office 3.6 to 4.4 m, corpo 4.0 to 6.0 m (a double-height lobby door where the ground floor holds it), security 2.6 to 3.0 m, industrial 2.8 to 3.4 m, each capped by the ground floor's clear height; the ground storey keeps the room for its row's shortest door whenever the envelope leaves it. Every entrance is 2.6 to 3.2 m wide, a grand entrance (hotel, corpo and venues at rich and high_rich) 3.2 to 4.8 m. A standard-family window (residential, hotel, office, corpo, hospital, commerce) is 70 to 90 percent of the clear height by its family row on a 0.3 to 0.6 m sill; where sill plus glass would pass the clear height the sill drops to the row's floor first and the glass is trimmed last. Office, corpo and residential ground floors use the `podium` row: windows at most 2 m wide and 1.5 m tall on a 1 m sill. Ground residential, office, corpo, industrial and security windows fit up to two symmetric pairs per face around the entrance or face centre, with full solid piers; a narrow entrance-free face may carry one centred window. Entrances and apertures retain priority; ground fields use continuous concrete. Explicit commerce, restaurant, coffee-shop and mall floor kinds keep storefront glazing. Other venue or lobby ground floors take the `storefront` row instead: glass from a 0 to 0.3 m sill up to the clear height, one run per bay (per panel cell on a megablock), and balcony doors rise to the window head. `security` and `industrial` are the documented small-window exceptions, and the small deep scattered window is the poor tier's megablock alone, above its storefront. The generator enforces all of it (`E_INVARIANT`).

## Out
Ground windows publish `windowTreatment: {privacy: "shell-only", nodeId}`. Its named `ground-privacy:<opening-id>` node contains a closed matte backing and perimeter housing behind the authored curtain, retained in merged exports. Consumers keep it only on shells with no real interior. Original curtains remain unchanged; privacy nodes are excluded from the permanent `facade.wallDepth` fit. Ground windows never imply a furnished fake room.

Commerce and mall buildings use punched glazing: at most two entrance-face display windows at ground level and two broad windows per upper-floor face, with at least 1.5 m solid piers around reserved openings. Seeded widths are 4, 5 or 6 m, reduced only for fit. Other ground faces are solid. Explicit mixed-use commerce/mall ground floors receive the same frontage treatment; their upper program is preserved. Restaurants and coffee shops retain their storefront program. Suppressed windows remain suppressed.

Industrial and security windows also publish `exteriorCovering: {style: "metal-louvre", placement: "exterior", depth, standoff, material}`. Closed metallic slats and their supports sit in front of the glazing; standoff is measured outward from the facade skin. This permanent fitting is independent of interior curtains and conditional ground privacy.

`options.exteriorStyle` selects one of nine coordinated sets; omission selects by seed, use and tier. The resolved ID is `facade.exteriorStyle`. [Materials bindings](../materials/bindings/exterior-styles.json) own the named surface variants; `src/layout/exteriorStyle.ts` owns their geometry policy. Residential sets are `residential-salvaged` (heavy worn panels), `residential-weathered` (punched weathered panels), and `residential-modest` (maintained panels and slatted blinds). Premium sets are `premium-obsidian` (sharp dark opaque glazing), `premium-office` (clear curtain wall and slats), and `premium-mineral` (rounded concrete with punched glazing and slats). Civic sets are `civic-utility`, `civic-institutional`, and `civic-industrial`, with fitted service networks and muted construction finishes. Megablock windows remain exclusive to poor tiers; other tiers use standard punched windows. Residential service density is 0.85, 0.70, and 0.55 respectively; explicit service and clothes options remain authoritative. Clothing still requires a residential use and a fitted clear seat.

`options.shape: "rounded-box"` builds four long straight faces joined by circular returns, with at least eight segments per quarter and at most 10 mm chord error. Every floor, wall, slab, roof, facade band and opening uses this same fitted ring. `premium-mineral` selects it by default; explicit shapes take precedence. Core fit and parcel containment may require the existing box fallback; aperture-constrained buildings keep parcel faces exactly. Explicit full balconies on residential and hotel curtain-wall sets use punched glass facades to retain fitted access.

Blueprint: [schemas/blueprint.schema.json](schemas/blueprint.schema.json). Per floor (basements included): index, kind, elevation, height, CCW outline, openings (door | window | balconyDoor | openFront | aperture) positioned by outline edge + offset + sill, with exact curtain coverage and a compatibility state, pane grid, explicit damaged-pane state, spandrel band, door leaves, fitted door assembly and transom, open-portal clearance, balcony band link, material key. Plus shared balcony bands, signage, screens, mounted exterior lights, facade style, exact face grids and artifacts, fitted `facadeServices` route graphs, fire escape, roof artifacts with fitted antenna and mast assemblies, the deduplicated material key list and stable named variant requests in `materialVariants`. External mast attachment records follow [schemas/external-attachment.schema.json](schemas/external-attachment.schema.json). The service payload follows [src/facade-services/schema/output.schema.json](src/facade-services/schema/output.schema.json).

Floor `kind` slugs are atlas vocabulary verbatim: the parcel type itself (`restaurant`, `coffee_shop`, `commerce`, `mall`, `residential`, ...) on every typed floor, venue and ground floors included, so interior assigns real venue programs. The only slugs that are not atlas types: `lobby` and `entry` (non-venue ground floors), `basement`, and the special top floor of a tall rich hotel (`bar`) or corpo (`executive`). Request `floorKinds` pass through unchanged.

GLB shell:
- Binary glTF 2.0, one scene, named nodes: `floor:<index>/slab`, `wall:<floor>/<edge>`, `facade-panel-borders`, `facade-relief`, `facade-ribs`, `window-weathering`, `ground-privacy:<opening-id>`, `facade-artifacts`, `facade-services`, `window:<opening-id>`, `door:<opening-id>` (with `door:<id>/frame` and one `door:<id>/leaf:<n>` per leaf), `open-front:<opening-id>`, `balcony:<opening-id>` (same frame and leaf children), `balcony-band:<band-id>`, `aperture:<id>`, `anchor:<id>` (the wire mount, its origin the attach point), `roof`, `parapet`, `terrace:<floor>` (setback rings), `columns`, `roof-artifacts`, `facade-ac`, `bulkhead` (with `door:roof-bulkhead`, its `/frame` and `/leaf:0`), `base` (bottom cap), `signage:<n>`, `screen:<n>`, `light:<n>`, `fire-escape`.
- `options.glb: "merged"` swaps the node scheme for one mesh per material key and authored variant (`merged:<theme/kind/tier[#variant]>`), for runtime scale; door leaves, anchors and every `floor:<index>/slab` stay named nodes (the game swings the leaves and hangs wires on the anchors, the interior replaces the slabs) and the blueprint is identical either way. Default `"named"` is the canonical interchange.
- Empty inside except one separator plane per floor at its elevation, faced both ways (a one-sided slab is invisible through the glazing from below and the shell reads hollow). The `floor:<index>/slab` nodes are replaceable in both node schemes: interior re-emits them with stair and elevator holes under the same names.
- All triangles CCW front, outward normals; every mesh carries a NORMAL attribute (flat per face, matching the winding) so it shades correctly in any viewer. Every opening is a real hole in the wall band (grid-cut, watertight, no T-junctions), lined by a reveal back to the unit that fills it.
- A door is an assembly: `door:<id>/frame` holds the reveal lining and a mitred casing of three members in the canonical painted-steel `window-frame` material, and each swinging leaf is one node subtree, `door:<id>/leaf:<n>`, holding every mesh that moves with it, its glass pane included. The opening's `door` object publishes the selected set (`plain`, `layered`, `glazed-grid`, `industrial-ribbed`, `illuminated`), actual frame and recess dimensions, threshold and movement envelope. `doorRole` tells interior and navigation whether the opening is the main street entrance, a secondary public entrance or service access. The leaf node sits on its hinge (outer jamb, floor level), so rotating the node about its own Y swings the leaf. Loading shutters publish roller travel; other doors publish swing travel and the clear depth behind the threshold. Industrial ribs belong to the moving leaf. Illuminated strips belong to the fixed frame, use the canonical `light-fixture` key, and mark their GLB material with `extras.materialVariant: "strip"`; the surrounding housing stays `window-frame`. One coordinated set is selected per building role, then fitted to the accepted opening dimensions.
- `options.entranceLayout` defaults to `single`: one public entrance, plus applicable service doors. Explicit `repeated` fits hotel, office, corpo and commerce entrances into complete 8 m frontage cells, up to five doors. Copies share dimensions and set, keep full piers, and publish secondary access roles. A selected open frontage takes precedence.
- Premium hotel, corpo and commerce entrances select compatible illuminated, layered or glazed-grid sets by seed; glazed-grid requires glazing. Swing leaves carry front-mounted pull handles or levers in frame metal, near 1.05 m above the threshold, fitted inside the recessed opening. Hardware moves with its leaf. Roller shutters carry no swing hardware.
- Eligible commerce, restaurant and coffee-shop buildings support a permanently open street frontage. `options.openFront: "on"` forces it where a valid fit exists; `auto` selects 30 percent of eligible one- and two-storey buildings by seed; `off` keeps a door. Width is 55 to 75 percent of the face, clamped to 4 to 12 m and whole metre seams. Height is 2.5 to 4 m under the clear head band. `portal` publishes frame width and depth, 0.35 m recess, exact traversable width, height and depth; `accessRole: "main"` supplies the navigation connection. The wall is cut, the reveal and three-member frame use `window-frame`, the floor and threshold share Y = 0, and no leaf blocks the opening. Signs and entrance lights use the same opening centre and head.
- A window unit is a closed extruded frame ring with front, back, outer and inner boundary faces, real reveal depth, a mullion grid splitting the opening into `panes.cols` x `panes.rows` panes, and glass recessed behind the profile. Profile sections and pane limits are seeded once per building, so a facade is consistent and buildings differ.
- Window coverings have closed 8 mm perimeter returns outside the clear field, connecting the glazing to the covering's back. Side, head and sill faces enclose recessed edges while preserving the authored slat profile and travel. Ground privacy continues that housing to its backing.
- On a punched facade the glass fills the hole exactly and the frame ring straddles its edge: half the member over the glass, half over the wall, its back on the wall skin, so nothing shows between frame and wall or frame and glass. A curtain wall has no wall to sit on and its mullion is shared with the bay next door, so there the ring stays inside the bay and the glass is the field it leaves. Every frame member, jamb, mullion and door casing carries its map along its own length, so a jamb and the head it meets read as one rolled section.
- Windows and glazed balcony doors carry a fitted covering behind the glass. Sets with the `slat` finish use `venetian-blind`: one fixed-pitch tilted slat profile, cassette, support tapes and bottom rail. Other sets use `roller-shade` with two-sided fabric. `curtain.closurePercent` is travel down the clear glazed height, authoritative over `state`; slats retain physical gaps. Seeds select 0, 30, 50, 80 or 100 percent in clustered patterns. Explicit `options.curtains.overrides[].openPercent` sets complementary closure. All geometry remains behind the pane and inside the clear field.
- `facade.style` publishes the selected set's facade construction: `curtain-wall` is a continuous glazed skin per upper-floor face with thin mullions and opaque spandrels; `glass` is nearly flush punched glazing; `panel` has recessed units, ribs and floor bands; `megablock` has heavy relief and scattered small deeply set windows on poor tiers. Window material keys distinguish ordinary, office and opaque glass. Door glazing keeps its transparent `door-glass` alias.
- Floor bands wrap every convex building corner with a closed mitred join at the same depth and height as both adjacent face runs. A corner never exposes a wall-colored strip between band ends. Acute corners use a bounded bevel so trim cannot form an unbounded spike.
- An upper-floor curtain-wall face carries equal piers at both corners: the metre that does not divide the face is split between them, so two faces never meet on a wide band of bare wall. It is glazed corner to corner in bays that hang slab to slab: each bay opening carries `sill + height = the floor height`, its full opaque spandrel is `head` at the top of the bay, and vision glass fills the field below it. The lower compatibility field `spandrel` is zero.
- Every floor line wears an opaque band, published as `facade.slabBand`: `below` metres under the walking surface and `above` metres over it. Slabs and ceiling plenums remain behind it. Curtain walls put the whole band at the head of the bay below the line; punched facades also keep `above` at 0. Door transoms occupy remaining space above a 0.15 m head gap when at least 0.2 m fits. Spandrels are closed solid boxes from frame front to glass back, including every end face, in matte `column` material. Mullions use `window-frame`. Curtain-wall frames and panels supply their own depth closure. Apertures retain solid collars.
- `facade.panelPattern` and `facade.grids` retain the 2 x 1 m placement guide for window cells, facade attachments and partition anchors. They are independent of the visible finish. `facade.surfacePattern` publishes continuous concrete or actual 7 x 3.5 m panels with 20 mm joints from Materials bindings. Meshing fits complete visible panels with continuous remainder borders; no small-panel texture is stretched. `facade.groundMaterial` is always continuous concrete. Grids publish exact opening-free solid runs and centred anchors wide enough for a 0.12 m partition plus 0.02 m clearance on each side. Vertical ribs and columns start above the first-floor band; the ground facade has no repeated vertical trim. Fitted facade equipment remains published in `facadeArtifacts` with its face, dimensions, standoff and material.
- Condenser units: clusters of two to four housings on one bracket, one housing per metre module, hung on bare wall under a window or on the blank band between window rows, from the first floor up (the ground floor belongs to the street). Each is a closed casing with a recessed exact-material grille, modeled circular guard, spokes, fan blades and hub, on a shelf carried by two wall struts. The grille wears `ac-unit`; the casing, guard, fan and bracket wear `metal`. They are common on the poor and mid tiers and on homes and shops, rare on a corpo, and a face with no wall to hang a bracket on (a curtain wall) carries none. The cluster's box stays inside the parcel footprint the way a balcony does, and never covers an opening or a window; where the wall it wants carries relief (a rib, a floor band) the bracket stands proud of it and `standoff` says how far. Machine-checked before output.
- `facadeServices` connects existing condenser clusters to fitted junction boxes with dimensioned round pipe networks, and industrial faces may add a dimensioned rectangular duct route between two fitted service boxes. A selected face may carry one sparse 12- or 15-cable bundle through at least three bends into an explicit wall entry. The GLB builds every cable as its own strand with the published diameter, spacing and outward slack, held by supports no more than 0.8 m apart; the wall entry has a fitted faceplate and fasteners. Every endpoint names the artifact or box it touches, every other facade-parallel run has wall supports at no more than 1.2 m spacing, and every node publishes both facade-local and world positions. Routes derive from the published face frame and panel seams, clear openings, entrances, balconies, apertures, wire mounts, fire escapes, signs, screens and lights, and remain inside the parcel. Fixed limits cap networks, segments, supports, units, triangles, material keys and draw calls.
- Residential poor and mid facades may carry seeded clotheslines on opening-free solid spans. Each line has two wall-mounted supports whose tips are its exact endpoints; sheets, shirts and trousers hang below it without entering an access clearance. Window damage is off unless `windowDamage: "sparse"` is requested. That mode selects at most three windows at one per 40 candidates, with one selected when any candidate exists, publishing `fractured-pane` as solid collision or `missing-pane` as open collision. The frame and undamaged panes remain intact.
- An exterior light publishes its carrying `edge`, rear-face `position`, `[width, height, depth]`, facade `normal` and `standoff`. The rear face is flush with the wall or the outermost relief crossed by its seat. Geometry uses the facade normal as outward depth, world +Y as up and the edge tangent as width on every facade orientation. The housing uses `window-frame`; one exact 0..1 `light-fixture` quad covers only the outward front, so side and rear faces do not emit. Duplicate seats are discarded before output. Sign and screen placement treats each fixture seat as reserved.
- `facade.wallDepth` is how far the deepest opening unit on this building reaches behind the outline skin, in metres, measured on the built geometry: window reveals, frames, glass and curtain panels, door reveals and closed leaves (a closed leaf sits 0.12 to 0.175 m in), aperture collars. A consumer fitting the shell keeps everything it builds at least that far inside the outline. It reads 0.175 wherever a door leaf is the deepest unit and 0.3 to 0.5 on a megablock, whose windows sit deeper than the doors.
- The roof plane carries a stair-head cutout, `roof.bulkhead`: a rectangle centred on the top plate, square to the shared core frame, given as center, unit `axis`, width and depth. Its complete rectangle is reserved for the stair shaft and arrival landing. Its size follows the current stair riser comfort band, flight count, tread, landing and wall-thickness arithmetic in ../interior/schemas/core-feasibility.json, including one- and two-storey climbs. The threshold elevation is exactly `roof.elevation`; `doorNormal = [-axis.y, axis.x]` is the stair-a arrival side. Interior lands its stair head there; roof artifacts and the walk space around the housing stay clear of it. Null when the plate is too small for access plus clearance.
- Every roof artifact has a deterministic `id` and fitted `[width, depth, height]` box. HVAC and cooling towers have supported casings, ducts or louvers and guarded top fans. Water tanks, stacks and vents use closed cylindrical bodies, bands and bases. Solar panels have tilted faces and supports; dishes have a fitted bowl, rim, pole and feed arm; screens, helipads, pools and roof bars use their own complete assemblies. The artifact mix is selected by building use and seed, and an explicit `roofArtifacts: "off"` leaves a quiet roof.
- An `antenna` or `mast` roof artifact carries `mastAssembly`. The `whip` and `crossarm-mast` variants publish the exact mast, arms, four base supports, internal cable attachment points and connected cable paths in world coordinates. The GLB uses those same segments. `externalAttachments` exposes one omnidirectional whip endpoint or two directional endpoints on the highest crossarm for a downstream interbuilding cable fitter. Each follows [schemas/external-attachment.schema.json](schemas/external-attachment.schema.json): a stable ID, exact world position, orientation, optional horizontal unit normal and roof-plane clearance radius. Exterior owns only the fitted endpoint; Connections owns pairing, span shape, thickness, sag and collision checks. The fitted `size` contains the complete base and crossarm footprint, so placement keeps the assembly clear of the roof housing and parcel edge.
- The housing over the cutout is a room: four walls carrying real thickness, faced on both sides so they read solid from the roof and from inside, a roof slab over them, and a real door on the stair-a arrival face (`door:roof-bulkhead` with its `/frame` and `/leaf:0`, hung and swung like every other door). Its door opening and casing begin at the threshold. Nothing else stands on the roof inside its walk space.
- A fire escape is the exception, not the rule: only residential and industrial buildings at the poor and mid tiers, two to seven floors, on a constant outline, and on `auto` (the default) only a seeded few of those wear one; `on` forces it where the building is eligible, `off` never. It hangs on the longest edge that is not the entrance facade, and its platform stack stands where every floor it passes steps out of a real opening onto it, windows before balcony doors; a run stops at the first floor it cannot serve and a face that serves no two floors in a row carries none. The blueprint publishes `edge`, `fromFloor`, `toFloor`, `offset` (u of the stack) and `width`.
- Balcony slabs and rails are shared `balconyBands`, each with one floor, face run, depth, slab thickness, rail height, style and the door ids it serves. A door points back through `balcony.bandId`; one band may serve several doors without overlapping duplicate slabs. `bay` and `juliet` retain local landings. The selective `full` variant spans a compatible rich residential, hotel or office face from one 1 m corner margin to the other, uses one continuous slab and front rail, and remains inside the parcel. Its walking surface equals the published floor elevation exactly. On a curtain-wall office, a stable seeded phase selects alternating floors and fitted bays receive a 2 m glazed access door; glazing remains on both sides and the door head stops exactly under the opaque slab band. `options.balconyStyle` may force `bay` or `full` where eligible; `auto` uses family, tier and seed.
- Signage is modular: one letter, one 1x1 cell, an N-letter text is N cells. `orientation: "horizontal"` is a framed marquee band over the entrance; `"vertical"` is a blade sign protruding edge-on from the facade, lettered on both faces, each face reading left to right from its own side. Every non-blank glyph has a separate four-member metal case with real depth and the luminous atlas face recessed inside it; `glyphCase` publishes its fitted outer size, depth and inset. The case remains inside the letter cell and does not close the atlas glyph's internal holes. The orientation is decided by what fits the facade, then by building family and the facade's proportions, so a building always gets the same sign. A marquee, a logo plate and an ad screen are closed boxes standing off the wall: solid backs, so nothing reads through them from behind.
- Signs and ad screens land by scanning the face for a clear rectangle. A door, a balcony door, an aperture mouth and a wire anchor mount are never covered. Bare wall wins; when the face has none, the plate crosses relief (rib, column, floor band, spandrel) or, on a facade that is all glass, the glazing, and mounts proud of it: `standoff` on the blueprint entry is where its back plane sits. The scan tries the wanted size at the wanted place, then steps along the face and up and down, then shrinks, then relocates to the next face in the entrance ranking; a sign with nowhere to go is dropped rather than forced. Machine-checked before output. The frame plate wears the `signage` material; each glyph cell is a square quad on the `letter-atlas` material, UV-cut to its character out of ../materials' 8 x 6 row-major sheet (`index = charset.indexOf(char.toUpperCase())`, rect `[(index % 8) / 8, floor(index / 8) / 6, 1/8, 1/6]`). The charset is `ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVWXYZ0123456789-.,'!?:/&+` plus a trailing space; a character outside it reads as that space, which reserves its cell width and gets no quad. Sign text is a request parameter.
- Every material is named by the canonical key `theme/kind/tier`. Kinds include concrete-monolith, concrete-large-panel, window-grime-sill, window-grime-jamb, exterior-louvre, wall-trim, window-glass, window-glass-office, window-glass-opaque, window-frame, curtain, door, door-glass, balcony-slab, balcony-rail, roof, floor-slab, parapet, signage, letter-atlas, ad-screen, light-fixture, fire-escape, aperture-frame, roof-artifact, ac-unit, fabric and metal. The selected set's roles resolve through Materials bindings, with `door-glass`, `balcony-rail` and `balcony-slab` retaining their canonical alias keys. Their maps match the binding's underlying kind. `facade.materialPlan` publishes concrete, border and trim choices under palette `neutral-dystopian`; `materialVariants` publishes every used named selection. GLB `extras.materialVariant` carries the same selected variants, including keys-only exports. Authored local slots, such as a luminous door strip, retain precedence.
- Curtain-wall bays are the one opening whose height is the floor height exactly, not a 0.05 m quantum: quantizing there would leave a wall sliver under every slab.
- Tiled materials get world-scale UVs (1 UV unit = 1 meter, planar per face, U along the face's horizontal edge) so textures never stretch. The facade panel field starts at the inner edge of its centered solid border, and each storey restarts V at the inner edge of its lower border. Every horizontal surface of one building (roof, floor slabs, terraces and the housing's own slab) shares one grid: origin at the ground plate's corner, U along the core's axis, with roof joints parallel to the parapet and slabs aligned with the roof through the glazing. Opening and style dimensions are quantized to 0.05 m, positions to millimeters. A panel field may place its two equal border edges on half-millimeter coordinates when an odd millimeter remainder must be divided evenly; the field between them remains an exact whole count of fixed panels. Exact-placement materials (ad-screen, signage, letter-atlas and window glass) get exact 0..1 UVs over their quad (a sub-rect of it for a glyph cell), never a partial tile. V follows the glTF convention: v = 0 is the top row of the map, so an atlas cell's top edge is its smaller v.

Textures. The default export is a finished exterior: every key resolves through ../materials into real maps (basecolor, normal, occlusion, emission where the entry has one, plus its metallic and roughness factors, transmission and IOR for glass). Tiled entries carry a `KHR_texture_transform` scale of 1 / tiling worldSize over the world-meter UVs; exact entries get clamped 0..1 UVs and no transform. Concrete, columns, trim, frames, doors, decks and exterior lights use the named variants published in `materialVariants`; missing required variants fail with `E_MATERIAL_UNRESOLVED`. Other surfaces keep deterministic seeded variation. Emissive strength is the entry's own; nothing is damped here. `textures.mode` on the result says which mode the GLB carries:
- `external` (default): map URIs written against `baseUrl`, nothing embedded.
- `embed`: the maps packed into one self-contained GLB.
- `keys`: material names only, for a consumer that resolves them itself (the engine runtime).
With no materials database at the configured path, output falls back to `keys` and `textures.reason` says so, so the box still runs standalone.

## Errors
Thrown as `ExteriorError { code, message, details? }`:
- `E_SCHEMA`: request fails schema validation; message names the path.
- `E_FOOTPRINT_INVALID`: footprint self-intersects, has under 3 points, or zero area.
- `E_FOOTPRINT_TOO_SMALL`: usable area below the type's minimum.
- `E_ENVELOPE_TOO_LOW`: floors x the type's minimum floor height exceeds maxHeight; the message carries the exact numbers.
- `E_FLOORKINDS_MISMATCH`: floorKinds length differs from floors.
- `E_APERTURE_UNREACHABLE`: face index outside the footprint, base outside the vertical range, an aperture taller than the type's max floor height, or no legal floor split exists for the requested floor count; the message names the feasible count range.
- `E_APERTURE_INVALID`: cut polygon off its face plane or inconsistent with u/width/height.
- `E_APERTURE_OVERLAP`: two aperture cuts overlap on the same face.
- `E_SIGNAGE_TEXT_TOO_LONG`: the text needs more letter cells than the facade holds either way round; the message names both limits.
- `E_CORE_PLATE`: the floor plates on this lot share no position for the core the interior needs for this floor count and its exact storey heights; the message names the band reached and the nearest core size, so the parcel can be retyped or resized.
- `E_MATERIAL_UNRESOLVED`: the materials theme has no entry for a key the building uses, or embedded textures were asked for without the database on disk.
- `E_INVARIANT`: post-generation coherence check failed; exterior bug, report with the request.

## Invariants
- Roof housing: sized from ../interior/schemas/core-feasibility.json (stair column width or shaft depth by the interior's recipe, square, plus a metre), placed at the top plate's centroid on the selected core frame; the interior centres its stair under it.
- Module grid: `MODULE` (0.5 m) sizes storeys, openings and balconies where the envelope allows. The retained 2 x 1 m placement guide locates facade attachments and partition seats. Visible surface modules come from `facade.surfacePattern`; their UVs retain the Materials world size.

- Face i is the vertical quad over parcel footprint segment i -> i+1 (connections convention). Every face carrying an aperture keeps its wall exactly on that segment; shape variation (octagon, cylinder, pyramid, inset) applies to aperture-free buildings, setbacks only above the topmost aperture.
- Every floor plate shares one core placement. A plate is the floor outline inset by the facade depth the interior publishes for the style. Core length is computed from the actual one- and adjacent-two-storey climbs, using the published riser comfort band, whole flight count, tread, landing, wall thickness, riser, service and egress arithmetic. The preflight tries the standard lift row, compact lift columns, then a stair-only walkup within its floor cap. It starts on the longest ground-outline edge and, only when that frame cannot fit, sweeps the same 5 degree fallback frames as the current interior contract. The core band is intersected across every floor, so setbacks cannot pass independently at different positions. Massing aims half a metre beyond the bare fit for snapping and steps inward only while the shared placement remains. A lot with no placement is `E_CORE_PLATE`, named before anything is built.
- Every bridge, ac-tube and tunnel aperture yields exactly one `aperture` opening carving exactly the given cut, with a floor walking surface at exactly its base; that floor is tall enough to contain the aperture's full vertical extent. A wire anchor cuts no hole: the GLB carries node `anchor:<id>` with its origin at the attach point and the mount under it, a plate sized to the cut (0.3 to 0.6 m square, 3 cm thick) plus the lug the wire hangs from, in the `window-frame` material, standing proud of whatever the face carries there. A punched facade keeps the plate's footprint clear of openings; a curtain wall glazes straight across it, so the mullion grid runs whole. The per-floor elevation table in the blueprint is final; interior consumes it.
- Floor elevations are contiguous: `elevation[i+1] = elevation[i] + height[i]`; ground floor at 0.
- One opening owns one stretch of an edge: two openings on the same floor and edge never share any of it, whatever their heights, so a consumer reads each facade as a run of exclusive intervals. Min 0.3 m pier between openings. Every opening lies entirely inside its edge with non-negative offset and inside its floor's height (machine-checked before output).
- Every door publishes exactly one navigation role. Only doors publish one. Repeated public entrances share their main entrance's accepted dimensions and fitted set.
- Every `openFront` publishes one fitted `portal` and `accessRole: "main"`; no other opening kind publishes either. Its clear width and height equal the wall opening less the fitted frame, and its clear depth equals the recess depth.
- Every `facade.grids` entry closes exactly on its face length and storey height. Removing its equal borders leaves a whole number of 2 m panels horizontally and 1 m panels vertically. Its solid intervals never cross an opening. Every partition anchor is centred in the solid run whose width it publishes and accommodates a 0.12 m partition with 0.02 m clear to each edge.
- Every window and glazed balcony door publishes one covering. Its lower travel boundary matches `curtain.closurePercent`; it stays behind the pane and inside the clear glazed field. Same requests reproduce coverage, and explicit open percentages produce complementary closure.
- Every floor band is continuous through each convex outline corner. Its join closes the top, bottom and outer faces and shares the adjacent runs' material.
- Every roof bulkhead uses the shared core axis, reserves the complete stair-head rectangle, puts its threshold at `roof.elevation`, and carries its door on the stair-a arrival side perpendicular to that axis.
- Every marquee glyph case is larger than its luminous face, smaller than its letter cell, and deeper than its inset. Invalid assemblies fail with `E_INVARIANT`.
- Every exterior light anchor lies on its published edge and carries that edge's exact outward normal. Size components are positive and standoff is non-negative; an invalid fixture fails with `E_INVARIANT`.
- Every opening the proportion table covers is the size that table promises: entrance height in its family range and at least the standard width its edge allows, window height at or above its share of the clear height on a sill inside the family range, storefront glass reaching the clear height on a sill inside its row (machine-checked before output).
- The entrance goes to a street-facing edge at least 3 m long when one exists (true point-to-segment distance from the access point, never a corner-touching sliver), and its zone is reserved before any window fill.
- Every balconyDoor resolves through `balcony.bandId` to exactly one band on the same floor and face. Its width and depth match the band, its opening lies within the band run, and every band lists at least one real access door. Balconies protrude beyond the outline but stay inside the parcel footprint. An aperture-pinned or otherwise constrained face with no room keeps the window instead.
- Office curtain-wall balcony doors occur on one alternating-floor phase, retain glazing beside them, and stop under the same head band as the vision panels. `options.balconies: "off"` removes them and their bands.
- The structure never exceeds the parcel footprint; it need not fill it. Whole candidate edges are checked against every interval of a concave boundary, including massing rings, roof housings, roof artifacts and condenser runs, so an edge cannot leave and re-enter through a notch between otherwise valid corners.
- Every roof artifact ID is unique and stable for the same request. Every antenna and mast has one fitted assembly anchored to the published `roof.elevation`. Every internal cable starts at its matching published attachment, all paths terminate at the assembly junction, and all support and arm endpoints stay inside the accepted artifact footprint. External attachment IDs derive from the artifact ID. Their positions match the whip tip or highest crossarm ends, their orientation matches the fitting, and their positive roof-plane clearance stays inside the artifact reservation and therefore clear of unrelated roof equipment and the roof-access keep-out.
- Every facade-service segment joins published nodes, every endpoint resolves to a published artifact or emitted service unit, and every route support lies on its segment and reaches the wall. A cable-bundle profile builds exactly its published cable count as separate strands and applies its published slack on facade-parallel spans. Clothesline supports terminate on their line and every item attaches to it. Services and clothes remain clear of all reserved openings, fixtures and access routes and stay within the published service limits.
- Blueprint floors match GLB geometry exactly (same outlines, same opening rectangles).

Window weathering uses exact alpha decals in `window-weathering`, with named `runoff` and `stain` slots. Sill receivers are 3 x 0.9 m and jamb receivers 0.4 x 2.5 m, clipped to the carrying face, floor and actual opening cuts with matching UV crops. They stand 2 mm from the wall. Curtain walls carry no opaque wall decals; selection is seeded and less frequent on maintained tiers.
