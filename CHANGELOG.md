# Changelog

0.26.1: condenser grilles wear the `ac-unit` kind materials shipped for them, one picture per face, with the housing and bracket on `metal`. Frames, doors, roofs and floor slabs always resolve to their canonical variant, so a frame section reads as flat painted steel on every building and a deck is a solid surface with no joint to cut on a plate that is not whole tiles.
0.26.0: whole panels, corner to corner and floor to floor. A box massing takes side lengths in whole panel modules where the parcel holds one and the plate still carries a core; where the outline has to be the parcel's own, the wall map is scaled to the panel that face and that storey really carry, so no corner and no floor line cuts a panel in half, and ribs stand on the same panel. A curtain wall splits the metre that does not divide a face between its two corner piers, so two faces meet on equal piers instead of a wide band of bare wall on one side.
0.25.3: a punched window's glass fills its hole exactly and the frame ring straddles the hole edge, half the member over the glass and half over the wall with its back on the wall skin, so no gap shows between frame and wall or frame and glass. Frame members, jambs, mullions, door casings and bracket sections carry their map along their own length instead of across it, so a jamb and the head it meets read as one painted steel section. A curtain wall keeps its shared mullion inside the bay.
0.25.2: every horizontal surface tiles on the building's own grid. Roof, floor slabs, terraces and the housing slab take their UVs from the ground plate's corner with U along the core's axis, so a roof joint runs parallel to the parapet instead of at an angle across it, and one metre of surface is one metre of map everywhere. Contract test measures the map against the geometry on four fixtures.
0.25.1: the roof housing is a room. Its four walls carry real thickness and are faced on both sides, so a housing read from inside is no longer see-through, the doorway is lined back to a real door (`door:roof-bulkhead`, its casing and one swinging leaf), and the housing stands square to the core's own axis, the longest ground edge, which is the frame the interior lays its core in.
0.25.0: buildings wear condenser units. Clusters of two to four housings hang on one bracket, a housing per metre module, on bare wall under a window or on the blank band between window rows, from the first floor up: common on the poor and mid tiers and on homes and shops, rare on a corpo, none on a curtain wall, which has no wall to bolt a bracket to. Each is a housing with a grille face on a shelf carried by two struts, published in `facadeArtifacts` as kind `ac-unit` with its `standoff`, and built under the `facade-ac` node on the `metal` material. Nothing hung on a facade may cover an opening or leave its face or the parcel, machine-checked (E_INVARIANT); utility boxes now take bare wall too, instead of landing on a column or a floor band.
0.24.6: curtain-wall bays are whole metres of panes from their start (the pier after takes the remainder), so every mullion line runs at one pitch across a facade; the spandrel is a matte dark stone panel.
0.24.5: the roof housing is sized from the interior's published stair constants (a square the stair head fits in either orientation, plus a metre of room), so the interior can centre its stair under it.
0.24.4: glass panes on the module (metre columns, half-metre rows, from a one-panel corner pier), so every mullion stands on a grid line the interior can wall against.
0.24.3: balcony doors are double: two metres wide, two leaves.
0.24.2: megablock cells are whole metres with ribs on their seams and every scattered window on the grid (metre widths and slots, half-metre heights and sills); curtain-wall spandrels are whole half metres.
0.24.1: the module runs a metre along the facade and half a metre up: bays, window widths (to the nearest metre), entrances, piers and columns (one panel wide, on seams) sit on metre lines; lanterns sit in panel centres; door casings and window reveals wear the flat painted steel; a sign carries a dark border plate behind its face.
0.24.0: the facade module: storeys, bays, windows, sills, balcony doors and corner margins are whole half metres, the head band one module, so half-metre wall panels starting at every facade corner and storey line frame each opening without a cut tile.
- 0.23.4: a rib never lands on an opening or an aperture cut, the rule columns already followed, so a megablock's panel grid steps over its doors.
- 0.23.3: every outer frame member is a closed profile with both side faces, so a frame standing proud of the wall never shows its hollow from the street.
- 0.23.2: entrance fixtures are facade obstacles like ribs and anchors, registered before the sign and screen scans, so a plate lands clear of them and the overlay invariant proves it.
- 0.23.1: every window hole is lined from the wall skin back to the frame's back plane (the style's recess plus the glass inset), so flush glazing shows no open edge beside its frame.
- 0.23: every floor plate holds a core. The core's axis is the longest ground edge, and a plate's depth is its extent across it behind the wall (`facade.wallDepth` plus a 0.25 m lining allowance); a setback steps in only as far as leaves 8 m of that depth, a ziggurat stops terracing where the next plate would lose it, and a plate that cannot afford any setback stays the ground outline to the roof. Machine-checked on the measured wall depth (E_INVARIANT) and covered by the shallow-tower fixture, city-urbe-small's p0.

- 0.22: the blueprint publishes `facade.wallDepth`, how far the deepest opening unit reaches behind the outline skin (window reveals and frames, glass and curtain panels, door reveals and closed leaves, aperture collars), measured per building on the built geometry and proven against the GLB by a contract test. Merged GLBs keep every `floor:<index>/slab` as its own named node beside the door leaves and anchors, so the interior swaps slabs by name in either node scheme.

- 0.21: proportions by family. Entrances stand 2.4 to 2.9 m on residential, 3.2 to 3.8 m on venues and hospitals, 3.6 to 4.4 m on hotels and offices and 4.0 to 6.0 m on corpo as a double-height lobby door, capped only by the ground floor's clear height, and the floor stack keeps the ground storey tall enough for its row's shortest door whenever the envelope leaves the room; every entrance is 2.6 to 3.2 m wide, grand ones 3.2 to 4.8 m. Standard-family windows take 70 to 90 percent of the clear height on 0.3 to 0.6 m sills, balcony doors rise to the window head, and a venue or lobby ground floor is a storefront: glass from a 0 to 0.3 m sill to the head band across the whole bay, per panel cell on a poor-tier megablock. The machine check covers entrance width and the storefront reach.

- 0.20: wire anchors carry their mount. The `anchor:<id>` node sits at the attach point and holds a plate sized to the cut (0.3 to 0.6 m square, 3 cm thick) with the lug the wire hangs from, in the `window-frame` material, standing proud of the mullions, ribs or bands under it. A curtain wall glazes straight across an anchor, so the mullion grid runs whole and no wall shows through the skin; a punched facade keeps the footprint clear of openings, and signs and ad screens never cover a mount.

- 0.19.1: one opening owns one stretch of an edge. A curtain wall's glazing over a door head is that door's `transom` light rather than a separate window, so no two openings on a floor and edge ever share a run, and a machine check proves it (E_INVARIANT). The entrance height cap rounds down onto the 0.05 grid, so a ground floor squeezed under 2.4 m of clear height still gets the tallest door it can carry.

- 0.19: signage and ad screens scan for clear facade. One relief model (ribs, columns, floor bands) now feeds both the mesher and a per-face obstacle map, and a placement scan puts every sign and screen on wall that carries nothing: it steps along the face, shrinks, then relocates to the next face, and where a facade offers no bare wall it mounts proud of the relief or the glazing it crosses. A door, a balcony door and an aperture mouth are never covered, utility boxes keep off the signs, and the blueprint publishes `edge` and `standoff` per plate. A machine check (E_INVARIANT) proves the no-overlap.

- 0.18.1: wall-mounted plates are closed boxes. A marquee, a logo plate and an ad screen carry a solid back panel standing a centimetre off the wall, so a sign shows its face from the street and nothing from behind.

- 0.18: doors are assemblies. Each swinging leaf is one node subtree (`door:<id>/leaf:<n>`, `balcony:<id>/leaf:<n>`) holding every mesh that moves with it, glass pane included, with the node origin on the hinge so the game turns it about its own Y; the blueprint publishes `leaves`. The casing (`door:<id>/frame`) is a mitred ring of three members that never share a plane, machine-checked by a contract test, and merged GLBs keep leaf nodes out of the merge so they can still swing.

- 0.17: curtain-wall facade style for offices and corpo at rich and high_rich, published as `facade.style: "curtain-wall"`. Each face is glazed corner to corner in bays that hang slab to slab, an opaque spandrel band (0.75 to 1.05 m, on the window-frame material) covering every floor line and thin mullions splitting the vision glass into panes within the tier limit; the skin runs over the entrance and over apertures as a transom light. Bays carry `spandrel` in the blueprint, curtain states on a curtain wall stay mostly open so the interiors read through, and the tier facade style is now a family-and-tier decision.
- 0.16: proportions are published and enforced. schemas/proportions.json carries the opening size table (clear height allowance, entrance range, per-family window height share, sill and width, storefront row, megablock exception) and the generator reads it: entrance doors stand 2.4 to 3.2 m tall, punched windows take 55 to 85 percent of the floor's clear height on a 0.7 to 0.9 m sill (offices and corpo lower and larger), venue and lobby ground floors get shopfront glazing, and the small deep scattered window stays with the poor tier's megablock. Residential and hotel storeys grew to carry the glass, office and corpo bays widened, and a machine check throws E_INVARIANT when any of it drifts. The preview gained the street-eye camera at 1.7 m in front of the entrance.
- 0.15.2: signs carry real letters. Every glyph cell is UV-cut to its character out of ../materials' `letter-atlas` sheet (8 x 6 row-major, 47 characters, uppercase mapping, anything off the charset reads as the blank space cell), on the `theme/letter-atlas/tier` material. Both faces of a blade sign read left to right from their own side.
- 0.15.1: wall bands with no hole in them were emitted as a degenerate quad, so most of every facade carried no surface at all: the root of the "no walls visible" report. Full-height strips now get their real corners, a contract test measures the built surface of every wall band against its band area minus its openings, and window openings are cut out of the wall like doors so a recessed unit sits in a real reveal.
- 0.15: roof access is real. The roof plane carries a stair-head cutout published as `roof.bulkhead` (a rectangle on the plate's own axis, its housing height and its door onto the roof), the housing is built over it with a door hole, and roof artifacts keep clear of the cutout and the walk space around it. The random `bulkhead` roof artifact is gone: the structure replaces it.
- 0.14: modular signage, one letter per 1x1 cell. A text runs as a framed marquee band over the entrance or stacks into a blade sign protruding edge-on from the facade, chosen deterministically by what fits, the building family and the facade's proportions; the blueprint publishes orientation, cell size and depth. Cell counts replace the old marquee capacity computation, and E_SIGNAGE_TEXT_TOO_LONG names both limits.
- 0.13: facade styles across the tier spectrum, published as `facade.style`. Glass (rich, high_rich) keeps clean mullioned glazing nearly flush with the panels; panel (mid) sets the unit back behind a reveal with thin ribs and floor bands; megablock (poor) puts heavy ribs on the panel grid and scatters many small deep-set windows semi-irregularly inside the cells, with surface-mounted utility boxes published as `facadeArtifacts`. The panel module is the wall material's tile size, so map joints and geometry share one grid.
- 0.12: real window units. Each opening gets a frame profile standing proud of the wall with the reveal sides that reach back to the glass, a mullion grid splitting it into panes no larger than the tier's structural limit (published as `panes` on the opening), and glass recessed a few cm behind the profile. Profile sections and pane limits are drawn once per building. Frame and trim plates carry world-scale UVs like every other tiled face.
- 0.11: standalone runs roll their own seed. A request with no seed (and no `--seed`) gets a random 12-hex seed, the CLI prints the seed of every run, and the preview keeps the seed in view with a random-seed button, so any building regenerates exactly.
- 0.10: the preview shows the finished textured building at first load: the dev server serves the materials box at /materials, the viewer keeps the shipped materials (a flat kind-coloured look stays available for reading geometry), the key light sits low so vertical facades read instead of going black, and the camera frames the whole building from outside. Floor slabs and the roof underside are faced both ways, so a shell seen through its glazing from below reads solid instead of hollow.
- 0.9.1: every exported mesh carries a NORMAL attribute, flat per face and matching the winding, so walls shade solid in any standard viewer instead of reading black; contract test asserts presence and agreement with the triangle winding on every primitive.
- 0.9: the default export is a finished textured exterior. Every material key resolves through ../materials into real maps (basecolor, normal, occlusion, emission, with the entry's metallic and roughness factors, transmission and IOR for glass); tiled entries carry a KHR_texture_transform of 1 / tiling worldSize over the world-meter UVs, exact placements get clamped 0..1 UVs. Maps ship as external URIs against a configurable base path, `--embed` packs them into one self-contained GLB, `--keys-only` keeps the keys for a consumer that resolves them itself, and a missing materials database degrades to keys so the box still runs standalone. New closed error `E_MATERIAL_UNRESOLVED`.
- 0.8.1: floor kind slugs are atlas vocabulary verbatim (restaurant, coffee_shop, commerce, mall and every other parcel type, venue and ground floors included), so interior assigns real venue programs; lobby, entry, basement, bar and executive stay the only non-atlas slugs.
